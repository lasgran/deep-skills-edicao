# Storytelling for Serialized Vertical Micro-Dramas (AI-animated, dubbed pt-BR)

Goal: every episode is a **bounded state change** that pays something off and ends with a reason to tap the next one.

## 1. Core principles
1. **Engine first, episodes second.** A season is one repeatable mechanism the audience learns to anticipate (e.g. a "dreams notebook" where each episode fulfills one item; letters from the dead that must be delivered). Show the mechanism slowly and completely the first time; later runs pay off through recognition.
2. **Each run must change something**: who knows what, a relationship's position, a resource/deadline, an escape route. Test: shuffle two episodes; if nothing is lost, they are repeats, not escalation.
3. **Payoff before pressure.** Give a real, visible result (a wish fulfilled, a truth half-told, a door that opens) before opening the next question. "About to be revealed" is not a payoff.
4. **Layered secrets + dramatic irony.** Stack secrets (A hides from B, C hides from everyone); let the audience know more than a character (they know the caller is the brother; she thinks it's the wife). Irony sustains; surprise jolts.
5. **Alternate emotions on purpose.** Sad → comic → warm → suspense inside one episode. **The biggest joy comes right before the biggest fall.** Warm "breather" episodes are allowed between hook episodes if the next one returns hard.
6. **Filmable conflict only.** If a beat disappears with the sound muted, it was volume, not a beat. Use objects, glances, doors, hands and reactions.

## 2. Season planning template
```
TITLE / LOGLINE: <who> wants <what> but <obstacle>, while hiding <secret>.
ENGINE (repeating mechanism): ____   Audience expects each run: ____   Ends when: ____
PROMISE (feeling sold): ____ (e.g. bittersweet hope + light comedy + supernatural mystery)
A-PLOT (external goal + clock): ____   B-PLOT (relationship, mirrors theme): ____
RUNNERS (C, 1-2 beats/episode, comic or mystery): gossip neighbor, grumpy-but-good elder, phone calls
SECRET LADDER (reveal order, each one re-prices the last):
  S1 small/personal (ep 1-3) -> S2 relational (~25%) -> S3 identity (midpoint) -> S4 supernatural/core (~80%)
CLUE BANK: each reveal has >=2 earlier visible clues (ring, glowing eye, drawer, handwriting).
MILESTONES: hook block ep1-3 / strongest early turn ~ep 8-12 / midpoint reversal / all-is-lost (deadline hits)
  / finale = revelation + final image mirroring ep1's opening image.
EPISODE GRID (one row each): # | title-promise | question answered | question opened | payoff |
  emotion curve | cliffhanger type | new clue planted
```
Rules: rotate cliffhanger types (no two consecutive alike); write all episode cliffhangers before the scripts; write in batches of <=10 episodes against the grid (long runs drift: hooks repeat, characters wander).

## 3. Episode beat sheet (timestamp skeleton before any dialogue)
**90 s (single plot, 1-2 locations, 2-3 characters)**
| Time | Beat | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:03 | Stop | Image/line legible with no context; audio already running on frame 1 |
| 0:03-0:15 | Answer + set | Answer last episode's question (or deliberately skip it); state today's want |
| 0:15-0:50 | Friction | Two people, one hiding something; each exchange raises the price |
| ~0:54 (60%) | Turn | One change: choice, reveal, arrival. Value flips (+ to - or - to +) |
| 0:54-1:20 | Fallout / payoff | Pay the cost on screen; land the promised emotion |
| last 5-8 s | Button | Silence, then hook image/line, 1-2 s reaction, hard cut to black |

**3 min (A + B/runner, 4-6 scenes)**
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0:00-0:05 | Cold open on the exact frame of the last cliffhanger, or a dry in-media-res mid-scene (title card <=3 s) |
| 0:05-0:40 | Scene 1: answer/partial answer + new question (often raised by a side character: "Planning to stay long?") |
| 0:40-1:20 | Scene 2: comic relief or runner; the engine item gets set up |
| 1:20-2:00 | Scene 3: midpoint jolt that re-prices scene 1 (misunderstanding, confession, price hike) |
| 2:00-2:40 | Scene 4: payoff spectacle (the wish fulfilled, the party, the boat) = biggest joy |
| 2:40-3:00 | Scene 5: fall + two-layer hook (outside threat + inside distrust), or a warm closing image on breather eps |
Every scene must turn one value. Name it: trust, safety, hope, pride, love, truth.

## 4. Hook menu (first 3 s)
- **Bombshell line**: "Você é casado?" / **Object out of place**: a ring glowing in a drawer.
- **In media res**: a door slams on her face in the rain. / **Strange rule**: "When someone dies, what was unsaid falls from the sky."
- **Visual contradiction**: a sweet kid character's eye reflecting a secret. / **Payoff of last ep**: continue the exact frame.
- **Title-promise card**: "EP. 2 - The betrayal that saved a life" (a mystery the episode, or a later one, pays).
Test: freeze at second 3. Would a stranger with sound off ask "what happens next?" If they need backstory, recut.

## 5. Cliffhanger menu (rotate; most are best as an image + one short line)
| Type | One-line example |
|---|---|
| Revelation | She folds his jacket, finds the wedding ring. Drone. Black. |
| Reversal | The man who paid for the medicine is the one she just threw out. |
| Mystery gap / new clue | His eye glows red as he runs. |
| Overheard / misread | "Stop calling me, I'm not coming back": she thinks it's the wife. |
| Witness | The little brother at the door sees the tin of letters: "...É a letra da mamãe." |
| Arrival / intrusion | Phone shows "Daniel (Brother)": "Oi. Onde você tá?" |
| Deadline | "The doctors gave her two months." |
| Danger / collapse | Mid-laugh, she faints; the music stops mid-bar; heartbeat. |
| Decision / dilemma | She holds the envelope over the trash can. |
| Emotional squeeze (breather eps) | Two characters lock eyes on the night boat. No threat, pure want. |
Rules: cut on the question, not the answer, two seconds earlier than feels safe. **Mystery image + one short line** beats a long explanation (don't show the content of the secret letter; show the reaction). Two-layer hooks (external threat + internal suspicion) are strongest. You may delay answering a hook 1-3 episodes, but log the debt and pay it.

## 6. Character sheet (one per main; <=10 lines)
- **Look lock** (for AI consistency): species/fruit/gem motif, colors, signature item (beanie, raincoat, ring). Keep new characters clearly distinct from any reference series.
- **Want** (visible goal, ideally with a deadline) / **Need** (inner change they can't see yet).
- **Wound/ghost** (one specific past event), then **False belief** in their own words ("Charity is humiliation").
- **Flaw on screen** (behavior we can film: pride, refuses help, jokes to deflect).
- **Secret** + who gets hurt if it comes out / **What they protect** (sister, dignity, a promise).
- **Usual strategy under pressure** (hide / care for / trade / attack / flee) and what it costs.
- **Voice**: rhythm, pet words, what they never say; ElevenLabs default tags.
- **Season test**: which episode pressure-tests the belief, and what visible choice proves change (or refusal).
Side characters need one trait + one turn: the gossip who becomes an ally, the grump who lends the boat. For each pair: A wants B to ___ / A hides ___ / A's leverage ___ (and vice-versa).

## 7. Dialogue rules (dubbing + AI lip-sync)
- **One line per shot, <= ~12 words / ~3 s** (pt-BR ~2.5-3 words/s, an estimate). Split longer thoughts across shot-reverse-shot.
- **One character speaking per shot**; no overlapping lines; 0.5-1.2 s gap between lines.
- **Natural pt-BR speech**: "cê", "tá", "pra", "a gente", contractions; no written-register Portuguese, no translated idioms.
- **Subtext over statement**: deflect, answer with a question, use an object. Plain, on-the-nose lines only for earned confessions/climaxes.
- **No exposition dumps**; reveal backstory as a fragment under pressure, later as a full reveal.
- **Comedy needs a straight man and a beat**: setup line, reaction hold, topper ("Doesn't look married... looks *tired*").
- Reuse **callback lines** across episodes ("Just one." / "For Your Majesty").
- **ElevenLabs v3/v4 tags** before each line, in English, 1-3 per line: emotion `[sad] [warm] [annoyed]`, delivery `[whispers] [softly] [shouts]`, pacing `[pause] [rushed] [hesitant]`, reactions `[sighs] [laughs] [gulps] [voice breaking]`. Ellipses = trailing pause; CAPS = stress ("EU"). Avoid tags that fight the image (laughing over a closed mouth).
- **Captions**: 2-4 words per chunk, spoken word highlighted, bottom third above platform UI; must work muted.

## 8. Pacing rules
| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Shot length | 2.5-4.5 s typical; 5-6 s for loneliness/aftermath shots; ~15-25 shots per 90 s |
| Framing | ~80% MCU/CU; wide only to establish a spectacle scene or isolate (door slam, empty street) |
| Cuts | Hard cuts only; no fades between scenes; scene opens directly in MCU |
| Camera | Slow push-in or gentle pan on every shot; zero shake |
| Reaction holds | 1-2 s silent hold after every heavy line; the reaction is the payoff |
| Music | New cue per scene/emotion shift; ~-18 dB under speech, swell in pauses; arguments often with no music |
| Silence | Cut music (mid-bar if shocking) 1-3 s **before** the hook; ambient only (rain, crickets), then low drone |
| SFX | Sparse, motivated: door, sizzle, fabric, phone buzz, heartbeat, comic "pop" for runner entrances |
| Ending | Final 2 s reaction, hard cut to black, music cuts with it; no CTA |
| Color | Grade per scene emotion (golden warmth, saturated day, magenta sunset, blue night + warm practicals) |

## 9. Checklists
**Before writing**
- [ ] Pitch the theme/premise to the owner and get an OK before writing a full script.
- [ ] Engine item for this episode chosen; which question from last ep is answered, delayed, or skipped (logged)?
- [ ] One value that flips; the turn at ~60%; the payoff named; the cliffhanger written first.
- [ ] Emotion curve drafted (e.g. tense, comic, warm, joy, fall).
- [ ] Who knows what at entry and exit (secret ladder updated; one new clue planted).
- [ ] Shot budget: <=2-3 characters, 1-3 locations, no crowds/hand close-ups/hugs unless essential (AI cost and glitches; hands and doors morph).

**Before approving**
- [ ] Muted freeze-frame test passes at 0:03; first frame already has motion + audio.
- [ ] Every scene turns a value; nothing could be cut without loss; no line explains the previous line.
- [ ] Every line <= ~3 s, one speaker per shot, natural pt-BR, tagged; reaction holds marked after heavy lines.
- [ ] Silence placed before the hook; hook is image + <=1 short line; hard cut to black.
- [ ] Cliffhanger type differs from the previous episode; reveals have earlier visible clues (fair play).
- [ ] Characters stay in character (strategy, voice, look lock); side characters get a turn, not just a gag.

## 10. Common mistakes
- Recap openings and slow establishing wides (viewers swipe in the first 3-5 s).
- A hook with no causal link to the episode (decoration), or a twist that comes from nowhere.
- Long explanatory finale lines; over-explaining the cliffhanger.
- Two or more characters speaking in one AI shot (lip-sync and identity drift).
- Repeating the same beat bigger instead of changing its vector.
- Every episode the same cliffhanger type; or every episode pure pain with no comic relief.
- Revealing the core secret too early, or holding a hook debt so long the audience forgets it.
- Wall-to-wall music: no silence means the hook doesn't land.
- Written-Portuguese dialogue, literal translations, lines too long for the shot.
